Moving from iPhone to Android?
Keep your messages.

Before you switch, back up the iPhone to your computer and save every conversation with OpenExtract (free) as PDF, HTML, CSV or text files that open anywhere. Then let your new phone's setup tool bring over what it can, and turn off iMessage so new texts reach you. The archive on your computer is your safety net if the move misses anything.

Google and Samsung both offer setup tools that can copy texts from an iPhone to a new Android phone. What comes across depends on the phone, the app and the iOS version, so it's worth having your own copy that doesn't depend on any of them. This guide is about your messages and the photos in them. For contacts, call history and the rest, see Switching to Android without losing your iPhone data.

01

Make one last backup before you reset the iPhone

Plug the iPhone into your computer and back it up with Finder (Mac) or the Apple Devices app or iTunes (Windows). OpenExtract can also make the backup from its start screen. On Windows that still needs iTunes or the Apple Devices app installed. An encrypted backup holds more, so tick Encrypt local backup (in OpenExtract the box is called Encrypt backup) and write the password down.

02

Save every conversation

In OpenExtract, open the backup. If OpenExtract made it, or you've opened it before, it's in your list. If not, choose to open a backup folder on the start screen. Open Messages, click Select, then Select all, then save them. Choose separate files and HTML for an archive you can read in any browser. Do it again with CSV if you'd like something you can search in a spreadsheet.

03

Save your photos

The HTML pages from step 2 already show the photos from each thread, and keep them, with any videos and other files, in a folder beside each page. CSV and text files only name them. Now open Photos in OpenExtract and save them all. That gets the camera roll: the photos you took or saved, not every picture someone sent you.

04

Move to the new phone, then check

Use the transfer option your Android phone offers during setup. Afterwards, open the conversations that matter most to you and make sure they came across.

05

Turn off iMessage

Before you take the SIM out, open Settings on the iPhone, go to Messages (under Apps on newer iOS) and turn off iMessage, then do the same in FaceTime. If you no longer have the iPhone, Apple has a web page where you can deregister your phone number from iMessage.

06

Keep the backup folder

Resetting the iPhone doesn't touch the backup on your computer. Keep it, and the archive, somewhere you back up.

What it can't do

  • It doesn't put messages on the Android phone. OpenExtract makes an archive on your computer. It doesn't import into Google Messages or any other app.
  • CSV and text files don't show photos. A photo shows as its file name, like [Image: IMG_0042.HEIC]. Photos people sent you are in the camera roll only if you saved them on the iPhone, so keep the HTML pages from step 2, which show them, with their folders.
  • No iCloud. It reads backups on your computer.

Common questions

Can I read the archive on my Android phone? Yes. The HTML files open in any web browser, so you can copy them to the phone or a cloud folder and open them there.

Will my old iMessages show up in Google Messages? That depends on your new phone's transfer tool. OpenExtract doesn't move messages onto a phone.

Do I need to keep the iPhone afterwards? No. Once the backup is on your computer, OpenExtract needs only the backup folder, plus its password if you encrypted it.

What if I already switched? If there's still a backup of the iPhone on a computer, you can open it any time.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
