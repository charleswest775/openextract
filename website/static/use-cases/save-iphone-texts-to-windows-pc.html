Guide · 4 min

Get your iPhone texts
onto a Windows PC.

Back up the iPhone to your PC with the Apple Devices app or iTunes, then open that backup in OpenExtract, a free Windows app, and export your conversations as HTML, CSV or plain text files. You can save one conversation, a handful, or all of them at once.

Windows has no built-in way to copy texts off an iPhone, and the backup Apple's software makes isn't something you can open in File Explorer. OpenExtract reads that backup and turns the conversations into ordinary files you can keep, search and print.

01

Install the Apple Devices app or iTunes

Windows needs Apple's software to talk to an iPhone over a cable. Get the Apple Devices app or iTunes from the Microsoft Store, or iTunes from Apple's website. You may already have one of them.

02

Back up the iPhone to the PC

Connect the iPhone with a cable and tap Trust on the phone if it asks. In Apple Devices or iTunes, select the iPhone, choose to back up to this computer, and click Back Up Now. Ticking Encrypt local backup is optional; if you do, write the password down. OpenExtract can also make the backup itself from its Get my data button, once Apple's software is installed.

03

Install OpenExtract for Windows

Download it from the Windows download page and run the installer. The installer is signed by Charles West, the developer. If SmartScreen shows a notice for a new app, choose More info, then Run anyway.

04

Open the backup

Choose Explore my data and pick the backup folder, usually %USERPROFILE%\Apple\MobileSync\Backup (the Apple Devices app, or iTunes from the Microsoft Store) or %APPDATA%\Apple Computer\MobileSync\Backup (iTunes downloaded from Apple's website). Type the backup password if it asks.

05

Save one conversation

In the Messages tab, click a conversation, then click the export icon at its top right and choose HTML, CSV or Text. Pick a folder, and the file is named after the conversation and its dates.

06

Or save many at once

Click Select above the conversation list, tick the ones you want or click Select all, then click Export. Choose separate files, or a single file sorted by time, and a format. For one spreadsheet of every message, use the Export tab and choose Messages.

Which format to pick

  • HTML looks like a chat and opens in any web browser. Good for reading and printing.
  • CSV opens in Excel or Google Sheets, one row per message with the date and sender. Good for searching and sorting.
  • Text is one line per message, readable anywhere, even years from now.

What it can't do

  • No wireless or iCloud transfer. It works from a backup on the PC, made over a cable.
  • Photos stay out of the message files. A photo in a thread shows as a placeholder or a file name. To save the camera roll, use the Photos tab or the Export tab.
  • No PDF button yet. Open the HTML file in your browser and print it to PDF.
  • It can't put messages back on an iPhone or onto another phone. The files are an archive for your PC.

Common questions

Do I need iTunes? You need either iTunes or the Apple Devices app to make the backup, because Windows needs Apple's software to connect to the iPhone. After that, OpenExtract does the rest.

Do I need the internet? No. Making the backup and exporting the messages both happen on your PC.

Where do the files go? Into whichever folder you pick when you export. Nothing is saved anywhere else.

Does it get iMessages as well as SMS? Yes, iMessage, SMS and group chats.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
