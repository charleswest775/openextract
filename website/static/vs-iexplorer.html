OpenExtract vs iExplorer

iExplorer, from Macroplant, is iPhone transfer software for Mac and Windows, with a free demo and a paid license. OpenExtract is a free, open-source iPhone backup extractor. Both get messages, photos and contacts out of a backup. Here's where each one is the better pick.

Pick OpenExtract if
  • You want to export messages, photos or voicemails from a backup without paying.
  • You're on Linux, or want a tool whose code you can read.
  • A backup on your computer is all you have, or all you need.
Pick iExplorer if
  • You want to work with the iPhone itself: music, app files, disk mode.
  • You need calendars, reminders, voice memos or Safari bookmarks.
  • You have an older Mac. iExplorer lists macOS 10.10 or later; OpenExtract's Mac build needs Apple Silicon.
  • You want a vendor to call on for support.

Both read the same Apple backup format, so trying one doesn't rule out the other.

Is iExplorer free?

You can download it for free, but the free version won't export your messages, contacts or photos. iExplorer's demo has no time limit and lets you browse messages, contacts, voicemails, notes, call history and photos. Exporting them needs a license. In the demo, music transfers go 10 tracks at a time and disk mounting stops at 150 MB. Macroplant spells this out on its free vs paid page and in its help center.

A license is $39.99 for one, $49.99 for two or $69.98 for five, each for Mac or Windows. Macroplant says a license is a one-time payment that covers every update to the current major version, iExplorer 4.

OpenExtract has no demo and no license. Exports work from the first launch, for every message, photo and voicemail in the backup.

Where iExplorer wins

The phone itself.
iExplorer connects to the iPhone directly: copy music and playlists to iTunes, browse app folders, mount the phone as a drive. OpenExtract only reads backups.
More kinds of data.
Calendars, reminders, voice memos and Safari bookmarks. OpenExtract doesn't read those.
Older Macs.
iExplorer lists macOS 10.10 or later. OpenExtract's Mac build needs an Apple Silicon Mac.
Vendor support.
Licenses come with support 7 days a week and a 30-day satisfaction guarantee. OpenExtract has GitHub issues.

Answered honestly

Can OpenExtract read the same backups as iExplorer?

Yes. iExplorer reads iTunes backups, and OpenExtract reads the same backups, whether iTunes, Finder or the Apple Devices app made them. Encrypted ones too. You don't need to make a new one.

Do I need my iPhone connected?

Not for OpenExtract. It works from a backup on your computer, so the phone can be lost, broken or already sold. iExplorer's music, app-folder and disk-mode features need the phone plugged in.

Can OpenExtract copy music off my iPhone?

No. OpenExtract reads backups; it doesn't move music. That's one of the things iExplorer is built for.

Is OpenExtract really free?

Yes. There's no demo, no license and no paid tier. It's open source under the MIT license.
