Guide · 3 min

Export iMessage
to PDF.

Open your iPhone backup in OpenExtract (free), click the conversation, and choose PDF. You get the whole thread as a PDF, with photos, dates, times and who sent each message.

PDF export is built in from OpenExtract 0.8.0. If you have an older version, download the latest from this page first; your backups don't need to change.

01

Make a backup on your computer

Plug the iPhone into your computer with a cable and tap Trust on the phone if it asks. On a Mac, open Finder and click the iPhone in the sidebar. On Windows, open the Apple Devices app or iTunes. Choose to back up to this computer and click Back Up Now. You can tick Encrypt local backup for a fuller backup; just write the password down. OpenExtract can also make the backup for you: choose Get my data on its home screen.

02

Install OpenExtract

Free for Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows 10 and 11, and Linux. Download it here. No account, no trial, no card.

03

Open the backup

On OpenExtract's home screen, choose Explore my data and pick the backup folder. If the folder holds more than one backup, it shows each one with the device name and date so you can choose. If the backup is encrypted, type the password you set. It stays on your computer.

04

Export the conversation as PDF

In the Messages tab, click the conversation, point at the export icon at its top right, and choose PDF (with images). Pick a folder. The PDF is saved there, named after the conversation.

05

Or put several conversations in one PDF

Click Select, tick the conversations, and click Export. Choose Single file sorted by time, set the format to PDF (with images), and click Export. Leave Separate files on to get one PDF per conversation instead.

What it can't do

  • Only photos that are in the backup. With iCloud Photos and "Optimize iPhone Storage" on, some pictures live only in iCloud. The PDF names those instead of showing them.
  • Pictures, not videos. Videos, voice notes and other files show as a line with their name.
  • Very long threads make very long PDFs. Thousands of messages take a while to export and make a big file.
  • It isn't a certified document. If it's for a legal matter, read printing texts for court first.

Common questions

Does this work on a Mac? Yes. OpenExtract runs on Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows and Linux, and the PDF export works the same on all three.

Will the PDF show dates and times? Yes. Every message has its timestamp, and received messages show who sent them.

Can I put several conversations in one PDF? Yes. Select them, click Export, and choose Single file sorted by time with the PDF format.

Does it work for SMS as well as iMessage? Yes, and for group chats.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
