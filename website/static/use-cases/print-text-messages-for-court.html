Guide · 7 min

Print text messages
from your iPhone for court, free.

The iPhone can't print a whole conversation. The free way: back up the iPhone to your computer, open the backup in OpenExtract, save the conversation as a PDF, and print it. You get the whole thread, with the date, time and sender of every message, not a pile of screenshots. Whether a court accepts it is up to the court and the attorneys.

On the phone itself you can screenshot a thread or forward a few messages, but forwarded messages lose their dates and times. A long conversation takes dozens of screenshots, and they often cut off the dates. A backup on your computer holds the whole conversation, so the printout can too. Here's a sample printout (PDF, 2 pages), made from a made-up iPhone we use for screenshots.

This page isn't legal advice. It's for messages on your own phone. OpenExtract makes a printable record. It doesn't certify or authenticate anything. If you have a case, ask your attorney, or the court's self-help center if you don't have one, what format they need. Some cases call for a forensic examiner rather than a printout you make yourself.

01

Make a backup on your computer

Plug the iPhone into your computer with a cable and tap Trust on the phone if it asks. On a Mac, open Finder and click the iPhone in the sidebar. On Windows, open the Apple Devices app or iTunes. Choose to back up to this computer and click Back Up Now. You can tick Encrypt local backup for a fuller backup; just write the password down. OpenExtract can also make the backup for you: click Back up my iPhone on its start screen.

02

Install OpenExtract

Free for Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows 10 and 11, and Linux. Download it here. No account, no trial, no card.

03

Open the backup

Backups in the usual place show up on OpenExtract's start screen under Found on this computer, with the device name and date. Click yours. On a Mac, the first time, you'll see "macOS needs your OK before OpenExtract can list Finder's backups here" instead: click Allow access, turn on OpenExtract in System Settings, then come back and click Quit & Reopen, and the list appears.

If your backup isn't listed, click Open a backup folder and pick it. Finder, iTunes and Apple Devices keep backups in ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup on a Mac (hidden: press Cmd+Shift+G in the window that opens and paste that path), and in %USERPROFILE%\Apple\MobileSync\Backup or %APPDATA%\Apple Computer\MobileSync\Backup on Windows. If OpenExtract asks for Full Disk Access, follow its steps. If the backup is encrypted, type the password you set. It stays on your computer.

04

Find the conversation

On the backup's home page, click Messages. Conversations are listed newest first. Type a name in the search box at the top to narrow the list, then click the conversation to read it. iMessage, SMS and group chats are all there.

05

Save it as a PDF

Click Save conversation at the top right, choose Readable document (PDF), click Save, and pick a folder. It saves the whole conversation, even if you searched inside it. The file is named after the person and the dates it covers, like Ray Duarte, Jun 12 2025 - Aug 29 2025.pdf.

06

Print it

Open the PDF and print it, or send the file as it is if that's what was asked for. To print part of a long conversation, choose a page range in the print dialog.

What's on the printout

  • The conversation's name at the top, then every message, oldest first, up to the moment the backup was made.
  • The date and time of every message. Each day's date appears once, above its first message, and each message has its time, written like this: "Jun 12, 2025" and "3:30 PM".
  • Who sent each message. Messages from the other person are on the left with their name above them, and yours are on the right. It shows a name if the person was in the phone's contacts when the backup was made, otherwise their phone number or email. In a group chat, every message from someone else has their name above it.
  • Photos sent in the conversation, if they're in the backup. Videos, voice notes and other files show as a line with their name.
  • A small line at the end saying it was made with OpenExtract.

Times and time zones

The times are in your computer's time zone when you save. If the messages were sent in that same time zone, the times match what the phone showed. If your computer is set to a different time zone, every time shifts with it, and a late-evening message can land on the next day. The printout doesn't say which time zone it uses, so if a time matters, write it next to the printout, for example "all times Pacific".

For exact times, save the conversation as a Spreadsheet (CSV) as well. Each row has the exact time with its offset from UTC, like 2025-06-12T15:30:39.512345-07:00. The digits after the dot are fractions of a second, and -07:00 means seven hours behind UTC (here, Pacific Daylight Time), so the exact moment can't be misread.

What it doesn't show

  • The other person's number, if they're in your contacts. The printout shows their name. Their number and email are on their card in OpenExtract's Address book.
  • Page numbers. The PDF doesn't number its pages. If the court wants numbered pages or exhibit labels, add them the way your attorney or the clerk asks.
  • Messages deleted before the backup was made. They usually aren't in the backup. See recovering deleted texts for what can and can't come back.
  • Photos kept only in iCloud. With Messages in iCloud on, the iPhone can keep older photos only in iCloud, so they aren't in the backup. The printout names them instead.
  • A date range. It saves the whole conversation. Print a page range to print part of it.
  • Any kind of certification. It's not a forensic tool, and the file isn't signed or notarized.
  • iCloud backups. It reads backups on your computer, not iCloud backups.

Other ways to save it

The same Save conversation window has three other formats:

  • Web page (HTML) looks like the PDF and opens in any web browser. It keeps photos, videos and other files in a folder beside the page, so keep the two together. Print it from the browser with Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac).
  • Plain text puts each message on its own line with its date, time and sender, for example [Jun 12, 2025, 3:30 PM] Ray Duarte: Maren this is Ray Duarte, Hollis. Your own messages say "me". It names attachments instead of showing them.
  • Spreadsheet (CSV) has a row per message: the exact time with its offset, the sender, the text, whether you sent it, and whether it had attachments. It opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

Keep the backup

OpenExtract only reads the backup and never changes it. Keep the backup folder as it is after you print, in case someone asks for it or wants it examined. The backup's home page in OpenExtract shows when it was made, for example "Backed up June 28, 2026".

Common questions

Will a court accept this printout? We can't tell you that. It depends on the court, the case and the rules where you are, and the judge and attorneys decide. Ask your attorney or the court clerk what they want before you print.

Does it show the date and time of each message? Yes. Each day's date appears once, above its first message, and every message has its time, like Jun 12, 2025 and 3:30 PM, in your computer's time zone. The printout doesn't name the time zone, so note it if a time matters. A spreadsheet (CSV) export gives each exact time, with its offset from UTC.

Does it show the other person's phone number? If they were in your contacts when the backup was made, the printout shows their name, not their number. Their number is on their card in OpenExtract's Address book. If they weren't in your contacts, it shows the number or email the messages came from.

Can I print iMessages and SMS texts, and group chats? Yes, all three. Group chats show who sent each message.

Can I print just part of a conversation? OpenExtract saves the whole conversation. To print part of it, choose a page range in the print dialog.

Will the other person know I printed our messages? No. OpenExtract reads a backup that's already on your computer and contacts no one.

Does it change the backup? No. It only reads the backup. Keep the original backup folder as it is, in case someone asks for it later.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
