Guide · 4 min

Save a loved one's
voicemails.

If the voicemails were on the iPhone the last time it was backed up to a computer, they are very likely still in that backup. OpenExtract (free) opens the backup, lets you listen to each voicemail, and saves the audio to a folder you choose, along with a list of who called and when.

Take this at whatever pace you need. Nothing here changes or deletes the backup, so there's no wrong button to press. If listening is hard right now, you can save the voicemails first and play them another day.

You don't need the phone, and you don't need their Apple ID. You need the computer the phone was backed up to, or a copy of the backup folder. We wrote more about this in Recovering voicemails from people who have passed.

01

Find the computer with the backup

Usually the Mac or PC the phone was plugged into. On a Mac, backups are in ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup. On Windows, look in %USERPROFILE%\Apple\MobileSync\Backup (the Apple Devices app, or iTunes from the Microsoft Store) or %APPDATA%\Apple Computer\MobileSync\Backup (iTunes downloaded from Apple's website). Please don't delete or tidy anything on that computer yet.

02

Install OpenExtract

Free for Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows 10 and 11, and Linux. Download it here. No account, no trial, no card.

03

Open the backup

On OpenExtract's home screen, choose Explore my data and pick the backup folder. If the folder holds more than one backup, it shows each one with the device name and date so you can choose. If the backup is encrypted, type the password you set. It stays on your computer.

04

Open the Voicemail tab

You'll see each saved voicemail with who called, the date, and how long it is. Press play to listen.

05

Save them to a folder

Click Export audio and choose a folder. OpenExtract saves an audio folder with one file per voicemail, named with the caller and the date, and a voicemails.csv list with the date, caller, length and any transcript the iPhone made.

06

Keep copies in more than one place

Copy the folder to an external drive, to a cloud folder you control, and to a family member. Drives fail and accounts close. Several copies mean the voices outlast any one of them.

What it can't do

  • Only voicemails that were on the phone. A backup holds the voicemails the iPhone had saved when it was backed up. Voicemail kept only on the carrier's system, the kind you dial in to hear, isn't in a backup.
  • Encrypted backups need the password. There's no way around it, for us or for Apple. A notebook, a password manager or a note on the computer is worth checking.
  • No iCloud. OpenExtract reads backups on a computer, not iCloud backups.
  • No backup, no voicemails. If the phone was never backed up to a computer, there's nothing on the computer to open.
  • Some players won't open the files. The audio is saved as .amr files. If your usual player won't open one, a free player such as VLC usually can. OpenExtract doesn't convert audio to other formats.

Common questions

Do I need their phone or their Apple ID? No. You need the backup folder. If the backup is encrypted, you also need the backup password.

Will listening or saving change the backup? No. OpenExtract only reads it. The backup stays exactly as it was.

Are the transcripts included? If the iPhone made a transcript of a voicemail, it's in the voicemails.csv file next to the audio.

Can I copy the backup to my own computer first? Yes. Copy the whole backup folder, then choose Explore my data in OpenExtract and pick the copy.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
