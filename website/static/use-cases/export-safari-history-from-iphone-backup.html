Guide · 3 min

Safari history
from an iPhone backup.

Open an encrypted iPhone backup in OpenExtract, a free app, see the pages visited in Safari and Firefox on your Windows PC or Mac, and save the whole history as a spreadsheet. No iCloud, no account, no export limit.

An iPhone backup made by Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app can hold the phone's browsing history, but not in a form you can open: Safari keeps it in a database inside the backup, under a file name made of letters and numbers. You might want it to find a page you remember reading, to keep a record after the phone is lost or wiped, or to go through a family member's phone with their backup. OpenExtract reads it on your own computer, for free.

One thing first: Apple puts Safari's history only in encrypted backups. If your backup isn't encrypted, it has no Safari history to read. Step 02 shows how to make one that does.

01

Install OpenExtract

Download it for Windows or Mac (Apple Silicon) and install it. The Windows installer is signed by Charles West, the developer; if SmartScreen shows a notice for a new app, choose More info, then Run anyway.

02

Open an encrypted backup, or make one

Already have an encrypted backup from Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app? OpenExtract lists the backups it finds on the computer on its start screen, or you can choose Open a backup folder. Here's where iPhone backups are stored. Type the backup's password when it asks.

No encrypted backup yet? Click Back up my iPhone, connect the phone with a cable, tap Trust on it and enter its passcode if it asks, then tick Encrypt backup and choose a password you'll remember. If the iPhone already has a backup password, the backup is encrypted with that one. An encrypted backup also has your call history, which Apple leaves out of ordinary backups too.

03

See the history

Click Web history on the backup's home page. It opens on an overview: browsing over the last 90 days, the sites visited most, the hours and days of the week you browse, and how much came from Safari and how much from Firefox. Click a site or a day to list just those visits.

The list shows each page's title and address, its site, when it was visited and in which browser, newest first. Switch between All, Safari and Firefox, or type in the search box to find pages by title, address or site. Reloads of the same page within two minutes count as one visit.

04

Save it as a spreadsheet

Click Save all and choose a folder, or open Save to this computer on the backup's home page and choose Web history. Every visit goes into one CSV file with the date and time (in UTC), browser, address, page title, site and, for Safari, how many times the address was visited. It opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

What it can't do

  • Safari history from an unencrypted backup. Apple leaves it out, so there's nothing to read. Make an encrypted backup instead.
  • Other browsers. It reads Safari and Firefox for iOS. Chrome, Edge, Brave and other browsers aren't shown.
  • Bookmarks, tabs and passwords. It reads the pages visited, not bookmarks, the Reading List, open tabs or saved passwords.
  • Cleared or private browsing. History cleared before the backup was made isn't shown, and private browsing isn't kept in history.
  • Only part of the list. Saving writes every visit in the backup, whatever the list is filtered to; sort or filter the spreadsheet afterwards.
  • It only has what the backup has. Pages visited after the backup was made aren't in it. It doesn't read iCloud or an iCloud backup.

Common questions

Why don't I see Safari history? Apple puts Safari's history only in encrypted backups. If the backup isn't encrypted, OpenExtract has no Safari history to show, though Firefox history can still be there. Make a new backup with encryption turned on, in OpenExtract (Back up my iPhone, then Encrypt backup), Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app, and open that one.

Does it read Chrome, Edge or Brave? No. It reads Safari and Firefox for iOS. History from Chrome, Edge, Brave and other browsers on the iPhone isn't shown.

What's in the saved file? One CSV spreadsheet, browser_history_export.csv, with a row per visit: the date and time (in UTC), the browser, the address, the page title, the site and, for Safari, how many times that address was visited. It opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

Are bookmarks, open tabs or saved passwords included? No. OpenExtract reads the history of pages visited. Bookmarks, the Reading List, open tabs and saved passwords aren't shown or saved.

Can it get back history I cleared? No. History you cleared in Safari or Firefox before the backup was made isn't shown, and pages opened in private browsing aren't kept in history in the first place.

Can I search the history? Yes. The search box finds pages by title, address or site, and the search on the backup's home page looks through web history along with messages, notes and the rest. Visits also appear in the timeline by date.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. It goes online only when you let it: to check for a new version, if you allow it (you can turn that off in Settings); to send a problem report or a thank-you, when you click Send; and on Windows, to install Apple's free Apple Devices app from Microsoft, if you ask it to, for backing up an iPhone. Nothing from inside your backup goes with any of them.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
