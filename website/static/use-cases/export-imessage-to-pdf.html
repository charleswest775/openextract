Guide · 3 min

Export iMessage
to PDF.

Today it's a two-step route: export the conversation from your iPhone backup with OpenExtract (free) as an HTML file, then open that file in your web browser, choose Print, and save it as a PDF. OpenExtract doesn't have a PDF button for messages yet.

A built-in PDF export for conversations is being worked on and is planned for v0.8. Until that version is out, the browser route below works on any computer and gives you the whole conversation, with dates, times and senders.

01

Make a backup on your computer

Plug the iPhone into your computer with a cable and tap Trust on the phone if it asks. On a Mac, open Finder and click the iPhone in the sidebar. On Windows, open the Apple Devices app or iTunes. Choose to back up to this computer and click Back Up Now. You can tick Encrypt local backup for a fuller backup; just write the password down. OpenExtract can also make the backup for you: choose Get my data on its home screen.

02

Install OpenExtract

Free for Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows 10 and 11, and Linux. Download it here. No account, no trial, no card.

03

Open the backup

On OpenExtract's home screen, choose Explore my data and pick the backup folder. If the folder holds more than one backup, it shows each one with the device name and date so you can choose. If the backup is encrypted, type the password you set. It stays on your computer.

04

Export the conversation as HTML

In the Messages tab, click the conversation, click the export icon at its top right, and choose HTML. Pick a folder. To put several conversations into one file, click Select, tick them, click Export and choose a single file sorted by time.

05

Open it in your browser and print

Double-click the HTML file. It opens in your web browser as a chat. Press Ctrl+P, or Cmd+P on a Mac. To keep the bubble colors, turn on Background graphics in the print settings if your browser has it.

06

Save as PDF

In Chrome or Edge, set the destination to Save as PDF. On Windows you can also pick Microsoft Print to PDF. On a Mac, click PDF at the bottom of the print window and choose Save as PDF. Name the file and save.

What it can't do

  • No PDF button yet. The steps above are the route until the built-in export ships.
  • No photos in the PDF. Photos in the thread show as placeholders, because the HTML export doesn't include the images.
  • Very long threads make very long PDFs. Your browser may take a while to prepare thousands of messages. Printing a page range keeps it manageable.
  • It isn't a certified document. If it's for a legal matter, read printing texts for court first.

Common questions

Does this work on a Mac? Yes. OpenExtract runs on Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows and Linux, and every one of them can print a web page to PDF.

Will the PDF show dates and times? Yes. Every message has its timestamp, and received messages show who sent them.

Can I put several conversations in one PDF? Yes. Export them as a single HTML file sorted by time, then print that file to PDF.

Does it work for SMS as well as iMessage? Yes, and for group chats.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier. The code is on GitHub.
