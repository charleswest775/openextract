Privacy

Privacy policy

OpenExtract is a desktop app that reads iPhone and iPad backups stored on your own computer. It is made by Charles West. Last updated November 11, 2026.

What the app reads

When you open a backup, OpenExtract reads the personal information in it, such as messages, photos, voicemails, call history, contacts, notes and browsing history, so it can show them to you and save copies where you choose. If the backup is encrypted, you type its password into the app to unlock it.

Where it goes

Nowhere. All reading and decryption happens on your computer. OpenExtract has no account, and it doesn't upload, sell or share your data with anyone. It doesn't save your backup password. While a backup is open, the app may keep temporary copies of the databases it reads on your computer. Files you export are saved only where you choose. The one thing that can leave your computer is a feedback report you choose to send, described below.

Network use

The app makes two kinds of network request, and only with your say-so. The first is an optional check for updates. The app asks you once before turning it on, and you can turn it off in Settings. A check downloads a small version file from GitHub, where OpenExtract's releases are published. It sends no information about you or your backups. GitHub sees the request, as it would any download. Updates download only when you choose to update.

The second is a feedback report, sent only when you click Send in the app's feedback form. See the next section.

Feedback reports

The feedback form shows you everything it will send before you send it: the type of problem, the part of the app, what you wrote, and a details section with the app and system versions, your phone's model and iOS version, and recent error messages. Folder names, your device's name, email addresses and phone numbers are replaced with placeholders, and nothing from inside your backup is included. You can edit or delete any of it. An email address is included only if you type one in, and it's used only to write to you about that report.

When you click Send, the report goes to openextract.app, which files it in a private list that only OpenExtract's developer can read. It isn't posted publicly. The app also sends a random code it makes for that report. The site keeps only a scrambled form of the code, so only your computer can ask about the report later.

While a report is open, the app asks openextract.app about it at most every few days, for up to six months, so it can tell you when it's fixed. Each check sends only the report's number and its code. The report number, its code, the email you typed and when you sent it are remembered on your computer.

The same form can send a thank-you instead. A thank-you holds only what you wrote, the part of the app you'd been using, and an email address if you type one in. It includes no details about your computer or your backup, and the app never asks about it afterwards. It only remembers, on your computer, that you sent one, so it stops asking. If you tick "OK to quote", your words may appear on openextract.app without your name, word for word, on the What people say page and a few others.

Before a quote appears, it is checked automatically. Only the words you wrote and the part of the app are sent to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, which reads them for names, contact details and private matters; your email address is never sent. If the check finds anything like that, or isn't sure, the quote stays off the site until OpenExtract's developer has read it. Anthropic doesn't use what it receives this way to train its models. If you'd like a quote of yours taken down, send another thank-you or a report from the app saying so.

Chipping in

The app's "Chip in" buttons only open openextract.app/support in your browser; the app sends nothing. If you pay there, Stripe handles the payment on its own page. OpenExtract never sees your card details. Stripe shares the amount, your email address and the name on your card with OpenExtract's developer, who uses them only to send receipts and refunds. See Stripe's privacy policy for what Stripe keeps.

No analytics in the app

The app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking. This website uses Vercel Web Analytics to count page visits, which is separate from the app.

Your control

Your data stays in your backup and in any files you export. Delete them whenever you like. Uninstalling OpenExtract removes the app.

Children

OpenExtract is a general-purpose utility and isn't directed at children.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. Questions are welcome as an issue on GitHub.
