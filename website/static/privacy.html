Privacy

Privacy policy

OpenExtract is a desktop app that reads iPhone and iPad backups stored on your own computer. It is made by Charles West. Last updated October 28, 2026.

What the app reads

When you open a backup, OpenExtract reads the personal information in it, such as messages, photos, voicemails, call history, contacts, notes and browsing history, so it can show them to you and save copies where you choose. If the backup is encrypted, you type its password into the app to unlock it.

Where it goes

Nowhere. All reading and decryption happens on your computer. OpenExtract has no server and no account, and it doesn't upload, sell or share your data with anyone. It doesn't save your backup password. While a backup is open, the app may keep temporary copies of the databases it reads on your computer. Files you export are saved only where you choose.

Network use

The only network request the app makes is an optional check for updates. The app asks you once before turning it on, and you can turn it off in Settings. A check downloads a small version file from GitHub, where OpenExtract's releases are published. It sends no information about you or your backups. GitHub sees the request, as it would any download. Updates download only when you choose to update.

No analytics in the app

The app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking. This website uses Vercel Web Analytics to count page visits, which is separate from the app.

Your control

Your data stays in your backup and in any files you export. Delete them whenever you like. Uninstalling OpenExtract removes the app.

Children

OpenExtract is a general-purpose utility and isn't directed at children.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. Questions are welcome as an issue on GitHub.
