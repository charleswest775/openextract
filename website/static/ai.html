OpenExtract AI · An add-on

Ask Claude about
your iPhone backup.

OpenExtract AI lets Claude, on your computer, search and read the iPhone backup there: years of messages, calls, voicemail, notes and photos. Ask in plain words. Each answer points to the message, call or note it came from, so you can check it.

What it reads

Everything below, from the iPhone backup on your computer, the same backup OpenExtract opens.

  • MessagesiMessage, SMS and MMS, group chats too, and the photos sent in them.
  • WhatsAppChats and groups that WhatsApp kept in the iPhone's backup.
  • CallsWho, when and for how long. Apple keeps call history only in encrypted backups.
  • VoicemailWith the transcripts the iPhone made, when it made them.
  • ContactsEach person's numbers and emails, merged into one person.
  • NotesApple Notes, whole.
  • CalendarEvents, past and coming, with yearly ones like birthdays.
  • PhotosDates, places and albums. Claude can look at a photo to describe it.

It only reads. It never changes your backup. The one thing it writes is a file you ask for, saved in a new folder. Every answer can cite refs, short labels like msg:12:925 or call:31 for the exact message, call, voicemail or note.

How you get it

  1. Open your backup in OpenExtract. It's free for Mac and Windows. No backup yet? Its Back up my iPhone button makes one.
  2. Click Ask AI, then Install for Claude. Ask AI is in OpenExtract's top bar when a supported AI app is installed. The add-on comes inside OpenExtract, so there's nothing to download.
  3. Start a new chat in Claude and ask. Your first three questions are free. Step-by-step help, for ChatGPT and Claude Code too, is in the setup guide.

What goes where

Your backup stays on your computer. What the AI reads goes to the AI company.

OpenExtract AI runs on your computer and talks only to the AI app that started it. OpenExtract sends nothing anywhere.

But whatever the AI reads from your backup, the AI app sends to the company that runs it, as it does with anything you paste into a chat: Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT. Ask about things you'd be comfortable sharing that way. More in the privacy policy.

Questions

Does it work on Claude's free plan?

Yes. Claude's free plan includes desktop extensions, which is how OpenExtract AI is added to Claude. A long question can read a lot of your backup, so heavy use may need a paid Claude plan. That's Claude's limit, not OpenExtract AI's, and OpenExtract AI's price is the same either way.

Which AI apps does it work with?

Claude's desktop app for Mac and Windows, first: that's what OpenExtract AI is built and tested with. ChatGPT's desktop app can connect too, and so can Claude Code. OpenExtract shows its Ask AI button only when one of them is installed.

Mac, Windows or Linux?

Macs with Apple Silicon, and Windows 10 and 11. There's no Linux add-on; OpenExtract itself still runs on Linux, free as always.

Does it work with encrypted backups?

Yes, and the password never goes to the AI. On a Mac, a password box from your Mac, not from Claude, asks for it, and Claude never sees what you type. On Windows the add-on can't show a password box yet; the setup guide says what works there. Never paste a backup password into a chat.

What counts as a question?

OpenExtract AI can't see what you type to the AI, only the lookups the AI makes in your backup. So it counts lookups that come within two minutes of each other as one question; a pause of more than two minutes starts the next one. Opening a backup and checking what's in it don't count. The three free questions allow up to 75 lookups in all.

Which backup does it read?

The one you last opened in OpenExtract. The AI can also list the other backups on your computer and open one when you ask.

Is OpenExtract still free?

Yes. OpenExtract stays free and open source, with no limits, whether or not you use OpenExtract AI. Nothing was taken out of it to make the add-on.

Can it be wrong?

Yes. It's set up to point to the message, call or note each answer rests on, to say when an answer rests on one message, and to keep plans apart from things that happened. Check anything that matters against the messages it points to.

What if it doesn't work for me?

Try the three free questions first. They run the same add-on a key unlocks, on your own backup, so you'll know before you pay. If something goes wrong, report it from OpenExtract's feedback form or on GitHub.

Who makes it?

The same people as OpenExtract: Charles West, an independent developer, with help from contributors. More on the About page.
