Guide · 4 min

Extract photos
from an iPhone backup.

Open the backup in OpenExtract, a free app, and save every photo and video in it to your Windows PC or Mac in one go: the original files, in a folder for each month, each dated the day it was taken. No iCloud, no account, and an ordinary backup is enough.

An iPhone backup made by Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app holds the phone's camera roll, but you can't just open it: the files are renamed to long strings of letters and numbers, with no names, folders or dates you'd recognise. That matters most when the phone is lost, broken or wiped and the backup is all that's left, or when you'd rather not restore a whole phone just to get the pictures. Most of the tools that come up when you search for this are paid apps. OpenExtract does it for free, with no trial and no limit on how many photos you save, and it reads everything on your own computer.

01

Install OpenExtract

Download it for Windows or Mac (Apple Silicon) and install it. The Windows installer is signed by Charles West, the developer; if SmartScreen shows a notice for a new app, choose More info, then Run anyway.

02

Open the backup, or make one

Already backed up with Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app? OpenExtract lists the backups it finds on the computer on its start screen, or you can choose Open a backup folder. Here's where iPhone backups are stored. If the backup is encrypted, type its password.

No backup yet? Click Back up my iPhone, connect the phone with a cable, tap Trust on it and enter its passcode if it asks, then choose a folder with room for your photos and start. Photos are in ordinary backups, so encryption is up to you.

03

Look through the photos

Click Photos on the backup's home page. The grid shows every photo and video in the backup, with favorites starred and the phone's albums listed alongside. Click a photo to see it full size, or use the menu at the top to show only photos, videos, Live Photos or screenshots.

04

Save them all

Click Save all and choose a folder. OpenExtract copies every photo and video in the backup into it, in a folder for each year and month (2025/07, 2025/08 and so on), and shows how far it has got: "Saving 1,234 of 5,678 photos and videos…". A big library takes a while, especially from an encrypted backup. When it's done, it says how many were saved, and how many are only in iCloud.

The same save is under Save to this computer on the backup's home page, next to messages, contacts and the rest; from there the photos go into a Photos folder.

05

Open them anywhere

Each file has the date it was taken as its file date, so Finder, File Explorer and photo apps sort them in the order you took them. Photos keep their EXIF details, such as the camera and, where the phone recorded it, the place.

Recent iPhones take photos as HEIC. Macs open them. On Windows, if a photo won't open, install Microsoft's HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store.

What it can't do

  • Photos that are only in iCloud. With iCloud Photos on, and especially with Optimize iPhone Storage, many full-size photos aren't on the phone, so they aren't in its backup. OpenExtract says how many; download those from iCloud instead.
  • The moving part of Live Photos. A Live Photo is saved as its still photo, without the short video.
  • One album at a time. You can browse albums, but Save all saves the whole backup.
  • It only has what the backup has. Photos taken after the backup was made aren't in it. Make a new backup first if you want today's camera roll.
  • No iCloud backups. It works from a backup on your computer, not from iCloud or an iCloud backup.

Common questions

Do I need an encrypted backup for photos? No. The camera roll is in ordinary backups as well as encrypted ones. If the backup is encrypted, OpenExtract asks for its password when you open it.

Why are some of my photos missing? With iCloud Photos on, and especially with Optimize iPhone Storage, many full-size photos live only in iCloud, so the iPhone's backup doesn't have them. OpenExtract counts only the photos that are in the backup and says how many more are only in iCloud. Those you download from iCloud instead.

Are the photos the original quality? Yes. OpenExtract copies the files as they are in the backup, so photos keep their format, resolution and EXIF details such as the camera and, where the phone recorded it, the place. HEIC photos stay HEIC.

Will the dates be right? Yes. The photos go into a folder for each month (for example 2025/07), and every saved file gets the date it was taken as its file date, so Finder, File Explorer and photo apps sort them in the order you took them.

Can I save one album? Not yet. You can browse each album in the app, but Save all saves every photo and video in the backup.

Does it work without iCloud? Yes. OpenExtract reads a backup saved on your own Mac or PC, so you don't need iCloud, an Apple ID sign-in or the phone itself once the backup exists. It can't read an iCloud backup.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. It goes online only when you let it: to check for a new version, if you allow it (you can turn that off in Settings); to send a problem report or a thank-you, when you click Send; and on Windows, to install Apple's free Apple Devices app from Microsoft, if you ask it to, for backing up an iPhone. Nothing from inside your backup goes with any of them.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
