Backup viewer

What's in your
iPhone backup?

Your computer may have years of iPhone backups on it. OpenExtract opens them so you can read every conversation, look through every photo and play every voicemail, without restoring anything to a phone.

An iPhone backup isn't made to be read. Finder and iTunes save it as thousands of files with scrambled names, indexed by a database called Manifest.db. You can't click through it, and Apple's only way back in is to restore it to a phone, which replaces whatever is on that phone.

OpenExtract reads that index and puts the backup back together, so you see it much the way you saw it on the phone: conversations as chat bubbles, photos in their albums, voicemails you can play.

What you can look at

  • Messages. iMessage and SMS conversations, group chats labeled by who said what, and the photos sent in them.
  • Photos and videos. The camera roll, with favorites starred and the albums made on the phone.
  • Contacts. The address book, with names, companies, numbers and emails.
  • Call history. Every call with its date, length and direction, including FaceTime.
  • Notes. Apple Notes, ready to read.
  • Voicemail. Saved voicemails with who called and when. Play them right there.
  • Safari history. The sites visited, from encrypted backups.
  • Timeline. All of the above in one list by date, filtered by type, person or date range.

When you want files instead of a screen, export conversations as PDF, HTML, CSV or plain text, contacts as vCards, notes as text or PDF, and voicemails as audio.

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no trial and no paid tier, and no cap on how much you look at or export. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, so there's no company behind it waiting to charge you later.

That's not true of every backup viewer. Some let you look for free and charge you to get anything out. iMazing's trial, for example, transfers up to 25 messages, 50 photos and 1 voicemail before you need a paid subscription, according to its trial FAQ and licensing policy.

Opening a backup
It finds backups on its own.
On a Mac or Windows PC, OpenExtract looks where Finder, iTunes and the Apple Devices app save backups, and lists what it finds.
Or point it at a folder.
A backup on an external drive, copied from another computer, or on Linux: choose its folder. Any folder with a Manifest.db in it works.
Encrypted backups open too.
Type the backup password. It's decrypted on your computer, and encrypted backups hold more, such as Safari history.
Your backup stays as it was.
OpenExtract opens backups read-only and changes nothing in them. Nothing is uploaded, and there's no account to make.

Questions

Do I need my iPhone?

No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your computer. You don't need the phone, your Apple ID or an internet connection.

I don't have a backup. How do I make one?

Plug the iPhone into a computer and back it up with Finder on a Mac, or iTunes or the Apple Devices app on Windows. Choose an encrypted backup if you want everything. How it works has the steps.

Can it open iCloud backups?

No. OpenExtract reads backups saved on a computer, not ones stored in iCloud.

Will it change or damage my backup?

No. It opens the backup read-only. You can view it as often as you like.
