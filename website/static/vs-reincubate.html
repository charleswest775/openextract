OpenExtract vs Reincubate

Reincubate's iPhone Backup Extractor is a Windows and Mac app with a free edition and paid licenses. It does a lot more than OpenExtract, including iCloud data and third-party chat apps. OpenExtract is free and open source, and does less. Here's where each one is the right pick.

Pick OpenExtract if
  • You want everything out of a backup on your computer for free, not four files at a time.
  • You'd rather not buy a license that runs for 12 months.
  • You're on Linux, or want code you can read.
Pick iPhone Backup Extractor if
  • You need data from iCloud, not just a backup on your computer.
  • You need WhatsApp, Viber, WeChat or Kik chats.
  • You need calendars, voice memos, Screen Time or location data.
  • You're dealing with a corrupt backup or a forgotten Screen Time passcode.
  • You want vendor support, or a commercial license for a business.

Both read the backups Finder and iTunes make, so you can try both on yours.

Is iPhone Backup Extractor free?

There's a free edition, and it's more useful than "free trial" might suggest. Reincubate's pricing says it previews every file in a backup and recovers 4 at a time, encrypted backups included. Its FAQ adds that you can recover everything by going through it four files at a time.

The paid edition removes that limit and adds iCloud Photo Library access, Apple Watch data recovery and Screen Time passcode reset. Prices start at $39.95. Reincubate's checkout lists that as 12 months on one computer, $59.95 for up to 3 computers, or $79.95 for up to 5, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. A Business edition, with a commercial license and priority support, is $299.95.

OpenExtract is free, with no file limit and no license to run out. It also does less: it reads backups on your computer, from Apple's own apps, and nothing from iCloud.

Same name, different apps

Reincubate's app is called iPhone Backup Extractor. OpenExtract is an iPhone backup extractor too, but the two are unrelated: different makers, different code.

Answered honestly

Is OpenExtract related to Reincubate's iPhone Backup Extractor?

No. They're separate apps from separate people. OpenExtract is an independent open-source project; iPhone Backup Extractor is Reincubate's commercial product.

Can either one read an iCloud backup?

Not OpenExtract: it reads backups saved on your computer. Reincubate's FAQ says getting data from iCloud backups hasn't been possible since May 2020, after changes Apple made, and points you to live iCloud account data instead.

Can OpenExtract open the backups I've been using with iPhone Backup Extractor?

Yes, if they're standard backups from Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app. Both apps look in the same backup folders, and OpenExtract can open a backup from any other folder you point it at.

Is OpenExtract really free?

Yes. There's no paid edition, no license and no file limit. It's open source under the MIT license.
