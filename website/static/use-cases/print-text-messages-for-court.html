Guide · 5 min

Print text messages
from your iPhone for court.

Back up the iPhone to your computer, open the backup in OpenExtract (free), export the conversation as an HTML or text file, and print it from your browser or text editor. You get the whole thread, with the date, time and sender of every message, not a pile of screenshots. Whether a court accepts it is up to the court and the attorneys.

The iPhone itself only lets you screenshot a thread or copy one message at a time. For a long conversation that means dozens of screenshots, and screenshots often cut off the dates. A computer backup holds the whole conversation, so the printout can too.

This page isn't legal advice. OpenExtract makes a printable record. It doesn't certify or authenticate anything. If you have a case, ask your attorney, or the court's self-help center if you don't have one, what format they need. Some cases call for a forensic examiner rather than a printout you make yourself.

01

Make a backup on your computer

Plug the iPhone into your computer with a cable and tap Trust on the phone if it asks. On a Mac, open Finder and click the iPhone in the sidebar. On Windows, open the Apple Devices app or iTunes. Choose to back up to this computer and click Back Up Now. You can tick Encrypt local backup for a fuller backup; just write the password down. OpenExtract can also make the backup for you: choose Get my data on its home screen.

02

Install OpenExtract

Free for Mac (Apple Silicon), Windows 10 and 11, and Linux. Download it here. No account, no trial, no card.

03

Open the backup

On OpenExtract's home screen, choose Explore my data and pick the backup folder. If the folder holds more than one backup, it shows each one with the device name and date so you can choose. If the backup is encrypted, type the password you set. It stays on your computer.

04

Find the conversation

Open the Messages tab. Type a name or number in the search box above the list of conversations, then click the one you need to read it. iMessage, SMS and group chats are all there.

05

Export it

Click the export icon at the top right of the conversation and choose HTML or Text, then pick a folder. HTML looks like a chat, with the sender's name above each message you received. Text puts one message per line, each starting with its timestamp and the sender. The file is named after the conversation and the dates it covers.

06

Print it, or save it as a PDF

Double-click the HTML file to open it in your web browser, then press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac). Pick your printer, or choose Save as PDF. A text file prints from Notepad or TextEdit the same way.

What's on the printout

  • Every message in the conversation, oldest first, from the moment the backup was made.
  • A timestamp on every message, written in full with its time zone offset, for example 2026-01-01T09:00:00+00:00. The +00:00 part is the offset from UTC. Check it, and say which time zone you mean if you quote a time.
  • Who sent each message. A name if the person was in the phone's contacts when the backup was made, otherwise their phone number or email.

What it can't do

  • No PDF button yet. To get a PDF, print the HTML file to PDF from your browser, as in step 6.
  • Photos don't print inline. In the HTML file a photo shows as a placeholder. The text file names each attachment instead.
  • No date range in the export. It exports the whole conversation. To print part of it, choose a page range in the print dialog.
  • It only has what the backup has. Messages deleted before the backup was made usually aren't there. See recovering deleted texts for what can and can't come back.
  • It doesn't certify anything. It's not a forensic tool, and the export isn't signed or notarized.
  • No iCloud. It reads backups on your computer, not iCloud backups.

Common questions

Will a court accept this printout? We can't tell you that. It depends on the court, the case and the rules where you are, and the judge and attorneys decide. Ask your attorney or the court clerk what they want before you print.

Does it show the date and time of each message? Yes. Every message has its own timestamp, including the time zone offset.

Can I print iMessages and SMS texts, and group chats? Yes, all three. Group chats show who sent each message.

Will the other person know I printed our messages? No. OpenExtract reads a backup that's already on your computer and contacts no one.

Does it change the backup? No. It only reads the backup. Keep the original backup folder as it is, in case someone asks for it later.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, no account and no strings attached. The code is on GitHub.
