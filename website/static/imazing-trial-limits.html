iMazing's free version and its trial limits

Yes, iMazing has a free version: its trial mode, which never expires. But in trial mode it copies out at most 25 messages, 50 photos, 10 contacts, 5 call-history entries, 5 notes and 1 voicemail. To get everything out, you can buy an iMazing subscription, from $49.99 a year, or open the same backup in OpenExtract, which is free and has no limits.

What happens at the limit

iMazing's trial FAQ puts it plainly: everything is accessible, but it restricts how many items you can transfer. The trial has no time limit, so it never runs out, but it never lifts the caps either. iMazing's getting-started guide says unlimited data exports need a license.

You can buy one in iMazing's online store, or inside the app from the Trial window or the License menu, and the license code is emailed to you. After that, the first time you use a premium feature with an iPhone, iMazing offers to link that iPhone to your license, and it takes one of the license's device slots.

Without a license, backing up your own devices stays free for personal use, and so does Quick Transfer.

How much does iMazing cost?

Since June 2025, iMazing has been sold only as a subscription, renewed yearly. Its store lists two plans for personal use:

  • Personal Subscription: $49.99 a year, for up to 5 iPhones, iPads or other Apple devices.
  • Personal Subscription, Family: $89.99 a year, for up to 20.

The store says prices are tax inclusive, and that you can cancel at any time, with the license valid until the renewal date. So if you only need iMazing for one job, you can cancel straight after and it won't renew. Students, professors and staff at schools and universities get 30% off with an educational email address.

These are the prices iMazing's store showed, in US dollars, on November 19, 2026.

Your two options

Both are fair choices. It depends on what you need from the backup and what else you want iMazing for.

Buy iMazing if
  • You need calendars or voice memos, which OpenExtract doesn't read.
  • You want to manage the phone itself: apps, music, moving to a new iPhone.
  • You want vendor support. The store lists email support 7 days a week.
Use OpenExtract if
  • You need messages, WhatsApp chats, photos, contacts, calls, notes or voicemails out of a backup.
  • You want all of it, not a sample.
  • You'd rather not start a subscription, or you're on Linux.

Open your iMazing backup in OpenExtract

If iMazing made your backup, you don't need to back up the iPhone again. It takes one step in iMazing first.

01

Know where iMazing keeps it

iMazing doesn't save backups where Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app do. By default it uses its own folder: ~/Library/Application Support/iMazing/Backup on a Mac, and %AppData%\iMazing\Backup on Windows, unless you picked another place in its settings. OpenExtract looks in Apple's folders, so it won't list an iMazing backup on its own.

Don't copy files out of that folder by hand. iMazing keeps several snapshots of each backup there and says to do everything through the app.

02

Export it as a normal backup folder

In iMazing, select your iPhone, or the backup in its Backups list, go to Tools and scroll to the Backup section. Click Export Backup as Folder, choose where to save it and confirm. iMazing writes the latest snapshot as a standard iOS backup folder, which other apps can read.

The same action is in the menu when you right-click the device on iMazing's Home Screen, under Backups. To export an older snapshot instead, open that snapshot in iMazing first.

03

Open it in OpenExtract

Install OpenExtract, click Open a backup folder on the start screen, and pick the folder you exported. Any backup folder with a Manifest.db file in it opens. If the backup is encrypted, type its password; it stays on your computer.

04

Save what you need, all of it

Save messages as PDF, HTML, CSV or text, WhatsApp chats as a web page, PDF, CSV or text, photos and videos as the original files, contacts as vCard or CSV, call history as CSV, notes as text or PDF, and voicemails as audio files with a list. There's no cap, and OpenExtract never changes the backup it reads.

Still have the iPhone? Then you can skip iMazing's backup altogether. OpenExtract can back up an iPhone connected by cable and open it straight away; on Windows it uses Apple's free Apple Devices app, or iTunes. Backups made with Finder, iTunes or Apple Devices work too, and OpenExtract finds those on its own. Where iPhone backups are stored has the folders.

Questions

Is there a free version of iMazing?

Yes: iMazing's trial mode, which has no time limit. Backups for personal use and Quick Transfer are free. What it limits is how much data you can copy out, such as 25 messages and 50 photos. Unlimited exports need a paid subscription.

Can I buy iMazing once instead of a subscription?

Not any more. In June 2025 iMazing stopped selling its device license and now offers only subscriptions, renewed yearly. You can cancel at any time, and the license stays valid until the renewal date.

Will OpenExtract open a backup iMazing made?

Yes, after one step in iMazing. Use Export Backup as Folder, which writes a standard iOS backup folder. Then click Open a backup folder in OpenExtract and pick it.

Is OpenExtract really free?

Yes. No trial, no limits, no account. It's open source under the MIT license, made by an independent developer with help from contributors. After an export, a note asks you to pay what it's worth to you, but only if you can.

What can't OpenExtract do that iMazing can?

It doesn't read calendars or voice memos, and it doesn't manage a phone: no app, music or ringtone syncing and no moving to a new iPhone. It reads backups on your computer, not iCloud backups. The Mac version needs an Apple Silicon Mac.
