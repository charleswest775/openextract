Moving to Android?
Keep your messages.

Before you switch, back up the iPhone to your computer and save every conversation with OpenExtract (free) as HTML, CSV or text files that open anywhere. Then let your new phone's setup tool bring over what it can, and turn off iMessage so new texts reach you. The archive on your computer is your safety net if the move misses anything.

Google and Samsung both offer setup tools that can copy texts from an iPhone to a new Android phone. What comes across depends on the phone, the app and the iOS version, so it's worth having your own copy that doesn't depend on any of them. This guide is only about messages. For contacts, photos, call history and the rest, see Switching to Android without losing your iPhone data.

01

Make one last backup before you reset the iPhone

Plug the iPhone into your computer and back it up with Finder (Mac) or the Apple Devices app or iTunes (Windows), or with OpenExtract's Get my data button. An encrypted backup holds more, so tick Encrypt local backup and write the password down.

02

Save every conversation

In OpenExtract, choose Explore my data and open the backup. In the Messages tab, click Select, then Select all, then Export. Choose separate files and HTML for an archive you can read in any browser. Do it again with CSV if you'd like something you can search in a spreadsheet.

03

Save the photos from your threads

The message files don't hold the pictures, so save the camera roll too, from the Photos tab or the Export tab.

04

Move to the new phone, then check

Use the transfer option your Android phone offers during setup. Afterwards, open the conversations that matter most to you and make sure they came across.

05

Turn off iMessage

Before you take the SIM out, open Settings on the iPhone, go to Messages (under Apps on newer iOS) and turn off iMessage, and FaceTime too. If you no longer have the iPhone, Apple has a web page where you can deregister your phone number from iMessage.

06

Keep the backup folder

Resetting the iPhone doesn't touch the backup on your computer. Keep it, and the archive, somewhere you back up.

What it can't do

  • It doesn't put messages on the Android phone. OpenExtract makes an archive on your computer. It doesn't import into Google Messages or any other app.
  • Photos aren't inside the message files. A photo in a thread shows as a placeholder or a file name, which is why step 3 saves the camera roll separately.
  • No iCloud. It reads backups on your computer.

Common questions

Can I read the archive on my Android phone? Yes. The HTML files open in any web browser, so you can copy them to the phone or a cloud folder and open them there.

Will my old iMessages show up in Google Messages? That depends on your new phone's transfer tool. OpenExtract doesn't move messages onto a phone.

Do I need to keep the iPhone afterwards? No. Once the backup is on your computer, the backup folder is all OpenExtract needs.

What if I already switched? If there's still a backup of the iPhone on a computer, you can open it any time.

Does anything get uploaded? No. OpenExtract reads the backup on your own computer and doesn't send it anywhere. The only thing it asks the internet for is whether a new version is out, and it asks you before it checks. You can turn that off in Settings.

Is it really free? Yes. OpenExtract is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier. The code is on GitHub.
